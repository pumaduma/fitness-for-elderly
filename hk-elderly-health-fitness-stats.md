## 1. Proportion of Hong Kong's population aged 65+

**A. Latest mid-year figure** | 1,718,900 persons aged 65+ out of a mid-year population of 7,524,100 (≈ 22.8%) | 2024 | Department of Health, "Health Facts of Hong Kong 2025 Edition" (compiled from C&SD data) | https://www.dh.gov.hk/english/statistics/statistics_hs/files/2025.pdf | About one in every 4.4 people in Hong Kong is now aged 65 or above. / 現時香港每約4.4人便有1人是65歲或以上長者。

**B. Official projection** | Elderly population (excluding foreign domestic helpers) projected to rise from 1.45 million (20.5%) in 2021 to 2.74 million (36.0%) in 2046 — "more than one in every three Hong Kong people will be an elderly" | Projection 2022–2046 (published Aug 2023) | Census and Statistics Department, "Hong Kong Population Projections 2022–2046" press release | EN: https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202308/15/P2023081500308.htm · 中文: https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202308/15/P2023081500304.htm | By 2046, more than one in three Hong Kong people will be aged 65 or above. / 到2046年，每3名港人中將有超過1人是65歲或以上長者。

## 2. Life expectancy at birth

**Latest (provisional)** | Male 82.8 years, Female 88.4 years | 2024 | Department of Health, "Health Facts of Hong Kong 2025 Edition" | https://www.dh.gov.hk/english/statistics/statistics_hs/files/2025.pdf | A Hong Kong man born today can expect to live to about 83 and a woman to about 88 — among the world's longest lifespans. / 香港男性平均預期壽命約83歲，女性約88歲，屬全球最長壽地區之一。

**Confirmed previous year** | Male 82.5, Female 87.9 | 2023 | C&SD, "Hong Kong in Figures 2024 Edition" (Table 15.1) | https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/data/stat_report/product/B1010006/att/B10100062024AN24B0100.pdf | Confirmed 2023 figures from the Census and Statistics Department. / 政府統計處確認的2023年數字。

## 3. Falls among elderly

**Hong Kong figure** | About 1 in 5 (≈20%) community-dwelling elders aged 65+ fall each year; of those who fall, about 75% sustain an injury (incl. head trauma and fractures) | 2013 (latest CHP compilation of HK data) | Centre for Health Protection / Department of Health, "Falls in the Elderly", Non-Communicable Diseases Watch, Nov 2013 | https://www.chp.gov.hk/files/pdf/ncd_watch_nov2013.pdf | Each year, roughly one in five older Hongkongers living at home will fall, and three-quarters of fallers are injured. / 每年約兩成居於社區的長者會跌倒，跌倒者中約七成半會受傷。

**WHO global figure (for context)** | An estimated 684,000 people die from falls worldwide each year; falls are the 2nd leading cause of unintentional injury deaths; adults over 60 suffer the greatest number of fatal falls; 37.3 million falls per year are severe enough to need medical attention | 2021 (fact sheet, 26 Apr 2021) | World Health Organization, "Falls" fact sheet | https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/falls | Globally, falls kill an estimated 684,000 people a year and older adults suffer the most fatal falls. / 全球每年估計有68.4萬人因跌倒死亡，60歲以上長者的致命跌倒個案最多。

## 4. Physical inactivity among HK older adults

**Not meeting WHO's recommended activity level** | Aged 65–74: 22.2% · Aged 75–84: 34.3% · Aged 85+: 53.3% · (All adults 18+: 24.8%) | Survey Nov 2020–Feb 2022 (report published 2023) | Centre for Health Protection, "Report of Population Health Survey 2020–22 (Part I)", Section 4.3.5 | https://www.chp.gov.hk/files/pdf/dh_phs_2020-22_part_1_report_eng_rectified.pdf | More than 1 in 5 Hongkongers aged 65–74, 1 in 3 aged 75–84, and over half of those 85+ do not get enough physical activity. / 本港65–74歲長者中逾兩成、75–84歲中三分之一、85歲以上逾半數運動量不足。

## 5. WHO physical activity recommendation for adults aged 65+

**Official recommendation** | For healthy adults 65+: (a) at least 150–300 min of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, OR 75–150 min of vigorous-intensity, OR an equivalent combination; (b) muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity involving all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week; (c) varied multicomponent physical activity emphasising functional balance and strength training at moderate or greater intensity on 3 or more days a week (to prevent falls); (d) limit sedentary time — "doing some physical activity is better than doing none" | 2020 guidelines | World Health Organization (wording as published on the HK Department of Health's Change4Health site) | https://www.change4health.gov.hk/en/physical_activity/guidelines/elders/ · WHO guideline document: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128 | Every week, 150+ minutes of moderate exercise (e.g. brisk walking), strength training twice weekly and balance training three times weekly protect older adults' health and prevent falls. / 每星期做至少150分鐘中等強度運動、兩次肌力訓練及三次平衡訓練，有助長者保持健康並預防跌倒。

## 6. HK government programmes relevant to elderly fitness

**A. Elderly Health Care Voucher Scheme** | HK$2,000 per eligible elderly person per year, with an accumulation limit of HK$8,000 (unchanged since 2020) | Current | Department of Health / Health Bureau, official scheme website (HCV) | https://www.hcv.gov.hk/en/hcvs/service_area.html | Every Hongkonger aged 65+ with a valid HKID gets HK$2,000 of health-care vouchers each year for private primary-care services (max HK$8,000 saved up). / 每位合資格65歲或以上長者每年可獲2,000元醫療券，用於私家基層醫療服務，累積上限8,000元。

**B. District Elderly Community Centres (DECCs)** | 41 DECCs + 173 Neighbourhood Elderly Centres + 1 Social Centre for the Elderly | As at 31 March 2025 | Social Welfare Department, "Community Support Services — 2024-25 Annual Service Provision and Statistics" | https://www.swd.gov.hk/en/pubsvc/elderly/elderly_info/elderly_ah_sps/elderlysps/csse/ | 41 district-level and 173 neighbourhood elderly centres across Hong Kong provide social, educational and recreational programmes that help keep older people active. / 全港共有41間長者地區中心及173間長者鄰舍中心，提供社交、教育及康樂活動。

**C. Community Care Service Voucher (CCSV) Scheme for the Elderly** *(note: the scheme commonly asked about as "ELCSS" — there is no official scheme by that name; this is the government's community-care voucher scheme)* | Made permanent on 1 Sep 2023; voucher quota rising from 12,000 to 16,000 in 2026-27; monthly voucher value HK$4,526–HK$10,824 (2026-27); users co-pay 5–40% of the package value according to household income (6 co-payment tiers); covers day-care, home-care or mixed-mode services | 2023–2027 | Social Welfare Department, official CCSV page | https://www.swd.gov.hk/en/pubsvc/elderly/cat_commcare/psccsv/ · overview: https://www.elderlyinfo.swd.gov.hk/en/ccsv_introduction.html | Older people assessed as needing care can "spend" a monthly voucher (up to HK$10,824) on day-care or home-care services of their chosen provider, paying only 5–40% themselves. / 經評估有護理需要的長者，可憑每月最高10,824元的社區照顧券，自選日間或家居照顧服務，只需自付5%–40%費用。

## 7. Loneliness / social isolation among HK elderly

**Primary finding (CUHK + Salvation Army survey)** | Proportion of elderly (aged 60+) reporting moderate or severe loneliness rose from 35.3% (2018) to 68.3% (2024) — nearly doubling; average loneliness score 3.56 out of 6 | 2024 survey (vs 2018) | CUHK Nethersole School of Nursing & The Salvation Army | CUHK: https://www.nur.cuhk.edu.hk/in-the-press/長者孤獨感較5年前升1倍　機構推治療小組助重拾正面心態/ · SCMP report: https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/health-environment/article/3264598/hong-kong-study-records-33-rise-elderly-people-struggling-loneliness | *(Label: survey of elderly users of Salvation Army services — indicative, not a territory-wide random sample)* More than two-thirds of elderly surveyed in 2024 said they felt moderately or severely lonely, roughly double the 2018 level. / 2024年調查中逾三分之二受訪長者感到中度至嚴重孤獨，較2018年幾乎倍增。

**Corroborating academic comment** | A small local questionnaire survey found nearly 60% of respondents aged 65+ felt extremely lonely | 2023 | HKU Li Ka Shing Faculty of Medicine (column by Dr Zohar Lederman) | https://www.med.hku.hk/zh-hk/media/knowledge-exchange/newspaper-columns/2023/july/living-together-through-lonely-times | *(Label: small survey, cited as approximate)* HKU Medicine notes loneliness is widespread, with a small local survey finding ~60% of older respondents feeling extremely lonely. / 港大醫學院亦指出，一項小型調查發現近六成65歲以上受訪者感到極度孤獨。

## 8. Cost of falls to the HK healthcare system

**Best available HK estimate (academic, territory-wide)** | Annual healthcare costs of falls in the 12 months after hospital discharge exceeded US$28.9 million (~HK$225 million); mean cost per faller US$11,129; mean cost per fall US$9,596 (~HK$75,000) | Study of 606,392 patients aged 65+ discharged 2007–2017 (published Oct 2023) | CUHK & HKU researchers, *Journal of the American Medical Directors Association* (Qian, Chau, Fong, Ho, Woo) | https://research.cuhk.edu.hk/en/publications/post-hospital-falls-among-the-older-population-the-temporal-patte-2/ · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamda.2023.07.014 | *(Label: covers post-discharge falls only — no official whole-of-HK cost figure is published)* Falls after hospital discharge alone cost Hong Kong's health system over US$28.9 million a year — about HK$75,000 per fall. / 單是出院後12個月內的跌倒，每年已耗用本港醫療系統逾2,890萬美元（每次跌倒約75,000港元）。

**WHO international benchmark** | Average health-system cost per fall injury for people 65+: US$3,611 (Finland) and US$1,049 (Australia) | 2021 | WHO "Falls" fact sheet | https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/falls | Internationally, each fall injury in an older person costs health systems roughly US$1,000–3,600. / 國際上，長者每次跌倒受傷的醫療系統成本約為1,000至3,600美元。

## Notes on gaps and labels

- **"ELCSS"**: No Hong Kong scheme officially bears this name. The closest official programme matching "elderly community care voucher" is the **Community Care Service Voucher (CCSV) Scheme for the Elderly**, detailed above. If the site team meant something else (e.g. Enhanced Home and Community Care Services), please confirm.
- **Falls — HK proportion**: The "1 in 5 per year" figure is the Department of Health's own published compilation (NCD Watch 2013), derived from local studies; it remains the standard citable HK figure. No more recent official CHP update of this exact number was found.
- **Loneliness**: No territory-wide official statistic exists (HKU Medicine explicitly notes the Government does not collect loneliness data). The CUHK–Salvation Army 2024 figure is the best recent citable number and is clearly labeled as a service-user survey.
- **Cost of falls**: No whole-of-Hong-Kong official annual cost figure is published. The JAMDA 2023 figure is the strongest available academic estimate and is labeled as covering post-discharge falls only.
- **Life expectancy 2024** figures are provisional (marked * in the DH publication); 2023 confirmed figures are provided alongside.
